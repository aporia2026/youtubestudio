Thumbnail redesign — “Every Computer Virus Type Explained in 8 Minutes”

Same topic, same video, two visual treatments. The left column recreates the aesthetic of the current thumbnail (dark, cinematic, 4-cell grid, thin serif labels). The right column applies the pattern that works on the channels actually winning this niche: bright flat icons, chunky bold labels, dense grid.

Channel-page size · 640 × 360

Mobile feed size · 360 × 203 (the size that actually matters)

What changed and why

  • Photoreal → flat illustration. The dark cinematic look reads as one murky panel on mobile. Flat icons on solid bright cells stay legible at 360px wide and don't carry the AI-generated visual signature.
  • 4 cells → 15 cells. Density is value. Every cell is a separate “lesson” the viewer feels they get for one click. The grid itself proves the “Every X” promise before anyone reads the title.
  • Serif thin labels → Arial Black uppercase. Labels jumped from ~14px equivalent to ~25px equivalent at full size, and from invisible to readable at mobile size.
  • Uniform dark cells → maximally-different bright cells. No two adjacent cells share a colour family. The grid reads as 15 distinct items instead of one blob.
  • Icon ring with black border. Every icon sits inside an off-white circle with a thick black stroke. That's what gives the reference channels their consistent “character collection” feel and what makes each icon pop off its background.
  • Short labels. “Crypto-Jack”, “Keylog”, “Ransom” instead of full names. The label is a visual anchor, not a dictionary entry — short words read at small sizes.
Cell colours rotated row-to-row so adjacent cells maximise contrast.

Caveats — what this mockup does not show

  • The redesign uses emoji as placeholder icons. The shipped version needs custom flat illustrations (a Trojan horse silhouette, a literal worm on the screen, a hooded RAT, etc.) so each cell has a unique drawn identity instead of a system emoji.
  • The recreated “current style” is my approximation, not a render of the actual thumbnail. The point is the aesthetic delta, not pixel fidelity.
  • The redesigned version is one example pattern. The same fixes — bright flat cells, chunky labels, dense grid, no dark fade — apply to “7 Levels of Dark Web”, “Ransomware Attacks”, and the rest. Each one needs its own grid logic.
